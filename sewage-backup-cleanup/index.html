Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Norwalk, OH

Backed-up drains and toilets bring Category 3 water indoors. Learn the safety steps and how cleanup is done.

☎ (567) 406-9153See all services

A sewage backup is wastewater coming up through a floor drain, toilet, tub or sink instead of going away. It is not a minor spill. The water is treated as Category 3 under ANSI/IICRC S500, meaning it can carry disease-causing organisms and should be handled with protective gear.

Toilets and drains are backing up into the house. What is that called?

If the question you typed was why the toilet bubbles, gurgles or fills the shower, the trade name for the aftermath is sewage damage restoration, and the cleanup is a Category 3 job. The first rule is simple: stop using water in the house, because every flush and every load of laundry sends more wastewater toward the blockage.

When sewage backup is an emergency

It is an emergency whenever wastewater is on a floor. Keep people and pets out, avoid touching the water and do not use a household vacuum on it. Turn off the electricity to the area only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water, and call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk before you call us.

Dispatch is open every day of the year, and same-day response is offered when crews are available. Leave cleanup to trained workers wearing gloves, boots, eye protection and respirators.

Why backups happen

  • A clog in the home's own lateral line, from roots, grease, wipes or a collapsed section
  • A blockage or surcharge in the public sewer that pushes water back up
  • Heavy rain overwhelming the system
  • A failed sump or floor drain, which is often the lowest outlet in the house

Norwalk owns and runs its wastewater treatment facilities, and the city's utility billing page lists a storm water charge on bills. If neighbors have the same problem, or the clog is beyond your property line, call the city so the public line can be checked. Otherwise a plumber clears and camera-inspects the home's own line.

How cleanup is done

Extract

Pumps and vacuums remove the water, which is disposed of properly.

Remove

Carpet, pad, insulation and soaked drywall are removed and bagged.

Clean

Surfaces are washed, then disinfected with products suited to the material.

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure to normal readings.

Anything porous that touched sewage is normally thrown away, including carpet and pad, upholstered furniture and cardboard. Hard materials such as concrete and sealed tile can often be cleaned. Structural drying finishes the job once the area is clean.

Sewage water does not stay in one room, so a quick call limits the spread.

Insurance and the water source

Insurers separate sewer and drain backup from other water damage, and many policies exclude it unless a backup endorsement was added. Check your policy or ask your insurer whether you have that coverage before assuming anything. Photos taken before cleanup, a list of discarded items and the plumber's report are the records an adjuster typically wants.

If the backup came with flooding from outside, flood damage restoration applies as well, and a wet basement is covered in basement water damage cleanup.

What affects sewage cleanup cost

Several factors move the scope:

  • Amount of material that must be removed and disposed of
  • Area and levels reached
  • Protective equipment and disinfecting steps
  • Drying days after cleaning
  • Rebuild of removed flooring and drywall

Sewer backup coverage is often an add-on, so confirm it. Want your real number? Call dispatch and describe the area affected.

Sewage Backup Cleanup questions in Norwalk, OH

Do you handle Sewage Backup Cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in Norwalk?

Yes. Sewage backups happen at night and on weekends, and dispatch is answered around the clock. Same-day dispatch of a crew is possible when one is free. Stop using sinks, tubs, toilets and the washer, keep children and pets out of the area and do not touch the water without gloves and boots.

What should I do if my toilet backs up and floods the bathroom?

Stop using all water, keep people away, avoid touching the water and call a plumber and the restoration line. Do not try to clean it with towels and a mop, which spreads contamination.

Is sewage backup dangerous to children and pets?

It can be, because the water may carry bacteria and viruses. Keep them out of the area until cleanup and disinfection are finished.

Who fixes the clog, the city or a plumber?

It depends where the blockage is. A clog in your own line is the property owner's repair. If the problem is in the public main, contact the city. A plumber with a camera can tell which it is.
