Milan is a village of about 1,371 people that sits in both Erie and Huron counties, roughly 7 miles north of Norwalk. The Huron River runs just north of the village square, and the village's 19th-century brick buildings, including the Kelley Block on the square, make it a place with a long history of living near water.

The river comes first in Milan

NOAA publishes a river gauge named Huron River at Milan, which is the quickest way to see how high the river is during a heavy rain. A homeowner with a basement near the river can check it along with the National Weather Service forecast. Milan's elevation of about 666 feet is lower than Norwalk's 715 feet, which is one reason low ground here can collect water after a storm.

The practical tie is simple: when rain is heavy and the gauge is rising, lift stored items off basement floors and make sure the sump pump has power.

When water is spreading in Milan

Rising water, a failed sump or a split line is an emergency. Close the main valve if the water is from plumbing, and stay out of a basement where water is near the furnace or panel. Call the dispatch line, which is open 24 hours a day, and mention whether you are in Erie County or Huron County.

The county matters because permit offices and some utilities differ. The village sits in two counties, so ask the village office which county rules apply to your address.

Steps to take in Milan before help arrives

Stop the source

Shut the main valve if the water is from a pipe, and note where it enters.

Protect power

Stay out of rooms where water touches outlets or the furnace.

Move belongings

Move rugs, boxes and electronics up off the floor.

Keep people clear

Keep children and pets away from river or drain water.

Milan's older buildings and services

Milan is known for preserved 19th-century architecture, the Thomas Edison birthplace museum and the Milan Canal, which opened in 1839. Old brick and stone foundations tend to take water at the joints, so the basement page and flood damage cleanup are the two pages most relevant here. When flood water has been in the house, sewage backup cleanup applies if drains also backed up.

Historic buildings call for care, so tell dispatch if a building is historic, protected or open to the public. Drying equipment is placed to protect floors, plaster and woodwork, and structural drying describes how readings are used.

Historic walls dry slowly, so measurements matter more than guesses.
