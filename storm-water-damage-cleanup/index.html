Emergency Storm Water Damage in Norwalk, OH

When a storm opens the roof or a window, rain gets in. Here is how storm water damage is dried and documented.

☎ (567) 406-9153See all services

Storm water damage is water that enters a building because wind, hail or heavy rain damaged the envelope: shingles, flashing, windows, siding or a tree-struck roof. It differs from flood water, which rises from the ground or a river and is covered on the flood damage page.

The intent here is water that came through the building, usually clean at first. A leak through the roof into a ceiling also appears on the ceiling page, which handles the interior repair.

When storm water is an emergency

It is an emergency when rain is still coming in, when a ceiling bulges or drips, when water reaches electrical fixtures or when a roof opening is large. Stay off wet roofs and ladders and keep away from downed lines. Emergency tarping by a roofer limits the water while repairs are arranged.

The line is staffed through holidays and weekends, and a crew can go out the same day if one is free. During a major storm many households call at once, so describe how much water is entering.

What to do while the storm is happening

Catch the drips

Place buckets, move furniture and lift rugs.

Photograph the damage

Capture the opening, the ceiling and the contents from several angles.

Avoid electrical hazards

Do not touch fixtures with water in them.

Cover openings safely

Tarp from inside or call a roofer for outside work.

Norwalk weather and what to expect

The June 2006 flood declaration that included Huron County, per the National Weather Service, is a reminder that regional storms can do real damage. The practical use of that fact is to check your homeowners policy for wind, hail and water coverage before storm season, not after.

Norwalk's zoning office lists roofs, siding, windows and shingles as exceptions to its exterior alteration permit requirement, so storm repairs to those parts do not need a city zoning permit under that page. Other exterior work may, so call the zoning office before rebuilding anything else.

How the cleanup is handled

A crew extracts water, checks insulation, ceilings and wall cavities with a moisture meter and removes materials that cannot be dried in place. Wet attic insulation is a particular concern because it keeps dripping into ceilings. Drying equipment then runs until the structure reaches the dry standard.

Storm Water Damage questions in Norwalk, OH

Do you handle Storm Water Damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Norwalk?

Yes. Storm damage is by nature after-hours, and dispatch answers around the clock. During a widespread storm, calls are handled in order of need and a crew is sent the same day when one is available. Cover openings only if it is safe, stay off wet roofs and keep away from downed lines.

Is storm damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Wind and hail damage that lets rain in is often covered. Rising flood water is usually excluded. Check your policy or ask your insurer what applies.

Should I tarp the roof myself?

Only if you can do it safely from the ground or inside. A wet roof is dangerous, so call a roofer for emergency tarping.

How soon should storm water be dried?

As soon as possible. EPA guidance is to dry water-damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth.
