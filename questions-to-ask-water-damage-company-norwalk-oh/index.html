What to Ask a Water Damage Restoration Company Before You Hire in Norwalk, OH

A good restoration company answers plainly. These questions help you compare companies and spot vague answers.

☎ (567) 406-9153See all services
By the Norwalk Water Damage Pros team

Hiring a restoration company is stressful because it happens while water is on the floor. Having a few questions ready makes it easier to compare options, even when you only have time to make two calls.

The questions below are grouped by what they reveal. You do not need to ask all of them, and a company that answers the first few clearly has already told you something.

Questions about the work itself

  • What will you do in the first visit, and how do you decide what to dry and what to remove?
  • Do you measure moisture and keep daily logs?
  • Will I get a written scope of work before work begins?
  • Do your crews work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage?
  • How will you tell me when the structure is dry?

A company that answers moisture questions with specifics, such as meters, daily readings and a stated dry standard, is describing a process. One that says it will see how it goes is not.

Be careful with any promise that sounds too certain. No one can guarantee how long drying takes before measuring, and no one can promise that an insurer will approve a claim.

Questions about who is on site

  • Are you a licensed and insured company, and can you show proof of insurance?
  • Do you use subcontractors, and who supervises them?
  • Who is my point of contact, and how will I reach them after hours?
  • Do you handle rebuild work or only mitigation?

Licensing depends on the state and the type of work, so ask whether Ohio licenses the specific work and ask to see the company's proof of insurance. For mold work, ask whether the state licenses it and verify on the state agency's site.

Norwalk permits and registration

Norwalk's zoning office says on its website that the city has no building inspector and no electrician for private property electrical inspections. Ask any contractor which approvals your repair needs, and ask the zoning office and Huron County building department to confirm.

Monroeville's village website lists zoning, permits and contractor registration on one page, and Willard's lists code enforcement and zoning. If you live in a village or city other than Norwalk, ask the local office whether contractors must register before starting.

Questions about what happens next

Scope

Ask for the written scope and read it before signing.

Daily readings

Ask how you will see them.

Change orders

Ask how added work is priced and approved.

Completion

Ask how and when the dry standard is confirmed.

If the loss involves sewage, sewage backup cleanup has extra safety requirements, and the company should explain protective gear and disinfecting steps. For standing water, water extraction describes what the first visit should include.

Questions that cover the work itself

  • Will you take moisture readings on day one and every day after
  • What equipment will you place, and how many pieces
  • How will I know when the structure is dry
  • What parts of the job do you subcontract, if any

A company that works to a standard should have plain answers. Industry practice is described in ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage and S520 for mold, and it is fair to ask if the crew works to them. Do not expect a lecture, only a direct answer.

Questions about paperwork

Ask for the scope in writing before work starts, and ask how changes to the scope are approved. Ask what records you will receive at the end, such as photos, readings and equipment logs. Those records matter when you talk to your insurer.

Ohio does not issue a general licence for water mitigation that this guide can confirm, so ask your city building department what applies to your project. See also water extraction and structural drying, and note that Milan and Collins callers can ask the same questions.

Questions homeowners ask

Should I get more than one estimate?

If time allows, yes, but do not let water sit while you compare. Start extraction and ask for a written scope you can compare.

Can a restoration company deal with my insurer for me?

A company can document the loss and share records, but you remain the policyholder and the one who decides. Be cautious of anyone who says you do not need to speak to your insurer.

Do I need a permit for water damage repair in Norwalk?

Drying usually does not, and larger rebuilds may. The city's zoning office and the Huron County building department can tell you.
