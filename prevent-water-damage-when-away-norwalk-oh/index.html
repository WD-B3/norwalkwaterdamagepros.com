How to Prevent Water Damage When You Leave Home for a Few Days in Norwalk, OH

A pipe that fails while you are gone runs until someone notices. A few minutes before you leave can prevent the worst case.

☎ (567) 406-9153See all services
By the Norwalk Water Damage Pros team

The costliest water losses often happen when nobody is home. A supply line or hose that fails in an empty house runs for days, and the house is soaked before anyone knows. A short checklist before a trip cuts that risk sharply.

The list below works for a weekend trip or a winter month away. Pick the steps that fit your home and your trip.

The main shutoff is the biggest step

If you shut off the main water valve, nothing can flow even if a hose fails. For a trip of a few days, many people do this at the main valve, then open a faucet briefly to relieve pressure. Know where the valve is, test it and make sure it fully closes before you need it.

Appliance hoses and ice makers

  • Look at washing machine hoses for bulges, cracks or rust at the couplings
  • Replace old rubber hoses with braided stainless steel hoses
  • Turn off the ice maker or its supply valve
  • Check the dishwasher supply line under the sink

A water leak alarm under a washer, water heater or sink is inexpensive and can send a message to your phone. If an appliance has already failed, the appliance leak page covers what comes next.

Winter trips: keep pipes from freezing

Water expands about 9 percent when it freezes, and a frozen pipe can split. Leave the heat on, at a modest setting, and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes. For a long absence, a plumber can drain the system.

Norwalk's cold and your pipes

Norwalk's NOAA 1991 to 2020 normals list about 33 inches of snow a year, and the record low is minus 25 degrees Fahrenheit. A few days of deep cold can freeze lines in an unheated crawl space, garage or exterior wall. Set the thermostat no lower than the level your plumber recommends, and ask a neighbor to look in during a cold snap.

Norwalk's water service is city-owned. If you shut off the water at the main valve inside the house, you do not need to contact the city. Changes to service at the meter are a different matter, and the city asks property owners to give notice for those.

A house that has been wet for days needs thorough structural drying, and the sooner it starts, the better. Mold can begin to grow in 24 to 48 hours in damp materials, according to EPA guidance.

Winter trips need extra planning

A house left cold can freeze a pipe in a single bad night. Set the thermostat no lower than a level you would trust on the coldest night, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls and ask someone to check the house if a hard freeze is forecast.

The frozen pipe page explains what happens when a line splits at the thaw. If you will be gone for weeks, consider draining the system or asking a plumber how to winterize the house.

Who should have a key

Leave a key with a neighbor, friend or relative who can walk in and shut the main valve. Show them where it is before you leave. A leak that runs for a week is a very different loss from one caught in an hour, and the difference is often just a person with a key.

If someone does find water, they should call dispatch first and describe it. Calls from Monroeville and Willard are answered by the same line.

Check hose bibs, sump pumps and gutters before you leave. A clogged downspout or a failed pump can cause trouble during a storm, and a quick walk around the house takes only a few minutes.

Questions homeowners ask

Should I turn off the water when I go on vacation?

For a trip of several days, yes. Closing the main valve removes the pressure that makes hose and pipe failures severe. Check your water heater instructions for vacation settings.

Is it safe to turn the heat down while I am away in winter?

Set it low but not off, at a level your plumber recommends. In very cold weather, a house left cold can freeze pipes.

Do I need to notify the city if I turn off my water?

Not for closing your own main valve. The city's utility office asks for notice when changes affect service at the meter.
