Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Norwalk, OH

Drywall wicks water upward and hides it inside the cavity. Learn how wet walls are measured, dried and repaired.

☎ (567) 406-9153See all services

Drywall is a gypsum core wrapped in paper, and the paper wicks water the way a paper towel does. A few inches of water on a floor can show up as a wet line a foot up the wall, and the cavity behind it can hold more water than the surface shows.

This page covers walls, drywall, plaster and baseboards. If the problem is a ceiling, see the ceiling page. Floor coverings are covered on hardwood floor repair and carpet restoration.

Signs that a wall is wet

  • Paint that bubbles, peels or turns brown at the base
  • Baseboards that swell, pull away or darken
  • Drywall that feels soft, crumbles or bows
  • A musty odor in a dry-looking room
  • A stain that returns after painting

A moisture meter and a thermal camera find wet material that looks dry. Pin meters tell you how wet a spot is, and the camera shows where the wet zone ends. Without them a repair often covers a wall that is still damp inside.

Cut, dry or replace?

Drywall decisions
ConditionTypical action
Wet for a short time with an intact surfaceOpen the baseboard, dry the cavity and repaint
Soft, bowed or stained throughCut and replace the damaged section
Water from a sewage or flood sourceRemove past the wet line and clean the framing
Insulation inside is wetRemove and replace, since it holds water against framing

A common cut line is a few inches above the visible wet mark, because water wicks above the stain. Cutting a flat horizontal line at a stud makes repair simpler. Baseboards are removed and set aside so that the wall bottom can dry.

How a wall is dried

After the baseboard comes off, small holes or a gap at the bottom let air movers push air into the cavity, and a dehumidifier lowers humidity in the room. Daily readings show when the framing is dry. Only then is new drywall installed, taped, mudded and painted.

Walls near an exterior door or window may involve storm water damage, and walls under a kitchen sink involve kitchen water damage.

Norwalk's finish and rebuild details

Norwalk's zoning office handles zoning permits for exterior alterations and lists siding, roofs, windows and shingles as exceptions. Interior drywall repair is a different matter, and the city's page says it has no building inspector, so contractors working on larger rebuilds should ask the zoning office and the Huron County building department which approvals apply.

The practical effect is that a small interior repair is straightforward, and a big rebuild is worth a call about permits before work starts.

Lead and older paint

Homes built before 1978 may have lead-based paint, and sanding or cutting old paint raises dust. If your home is older, ask the contractor about lead-safe work practices before the wall is cut. The EPA's renovation rule covers contractors working on pre-1978 homes.

What affects wall and drywall repair cost

Walls are priced by area and by what is behind them:

  • Height and length of the wet section
  • Insulation and framing that must be dried or replaced
  • Number of baseboards and trim pieces
  • Texture, paint and finish matching
  • Days of drying before rebuild
  • Whether the water source is repaired

Sudden water damage to walls is often covered by homeowners policies, but repeated seepage often is not. Check your policy or ask your insurer. Want your real number? Call dispatch.

Painting comes last

Paint and new baseboards go on only after readings show the wall cavity is dry. Painting early traps moisture and hides the problem.

Wall and Drywall Water Damage questions in Norwalk, OH

How far above the stain should I cut drywall?

A few inches above the visible mark is a common rule, but a moisture meter shows how far water has wicked. The meter, not the stain, sets the cut line.

Can I paint over a water stain on a wall?

Only after the wall is dry and the leak fixed. A stain-blocking primer covers old stains, but paint on a damp wall will fail.

Why are baseboards removed during drying?

So air can reach the cavity behind them. Wet baseboards also hold moisture against the wall bottom.
