Collins is a small, unincorporated community in Townsend Township, about 6 miles east of Norwalk along U.S. Route 20. When water gets into a home or outbuilding there, the call goes to Norwalk dispatch, which is answered every hour of every day, including nights and holidays.

When water is spreading in Collins

Water that is still moving is the emergency, whether it is a split line in a wall, an overflowing tub or rain driving in around a window. Shut the main valve, then call. Say whether you are on US 20, Hartland Center Road or a side road, because a clear route shortens the first visit.

Same-day response is offered when crews are available. No arrival time is promised on the phone, since a crew coming from Norwalk travels the same stretch of US 20 as everyone else.

While you wait for the crew in Collins

Stop the source

Close the main water valve and drain the lines at the lowest faucet.

Protect power

Switch off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable.

Move belongings

Lift furniture, papers and electronics onto dry surfaces.

Keep people clear

Keep children and pets out of any water that came from a drain or outdoors.

Why Collins calls are handled a little differently

Collins is a census-designated place that includes East Townsend, and the land around it is spread out. Rural lots often mean a long driveway, an outbuilding or a well, so tell dispatch if the water is in a barn, a detached garage or a basement under an addition. Ask whether your property is on municipal water or a private well, since a well pump failure is a plumbing issue and not a utility one.

The community's elevation of about 892 feet is higher than Norwalk's 715 feet, but higher ground does not stop a basement from taking water after a long rain. The relevant step is a working sump pump and downspouts that carry water away from the house.

Services Collins callers ask about most

Homes with finished lower levels will want the basement water damage page, and a split line in a wall follows the steps on burst pipe water damage.

Permits and who to ask

Collins is unincorporated, so there is no village hall to ask about repairs. Ask Townsend Township and the Huron County offices which approvals apply to your rebuild. Insurance repairs often involve contractors who need to know whether a permit is required before they order materials.

On coverage, many homeowners policies respond to a sudden pipe failure but not to flood water, so read the water section of your policy or call your agent before you decide anything.

Water damage questions in Collins, OH

Do you cover every ZIP code in Collins?

Collins uses ZIP code 44826, and the same crews also serve the neighboring ZIPs 44889 in Wakeman, 44855 in North Fairfield and 44814 in Berlin Heights, from the US 20 corridor east of Norwalk to the Erie County line. Tell dispatch your street and ZIP when you call.

Is emergency water removal available in Collins?

Yes. Dispatch is open 24 hours a day, and a crew is sent the same day when one is available. While you wait, close the main valve, lift belongings and keep people out of the water.

What should I know about a private well when water damage happens?

A flooded well or a failed pump is a plumbing and well-contractor problem, not part of drying the house. Tell dispatch which water source you use, and ask a well contractor to check the system before you drink the water after a flood.
