Mold vs Mildew: How to Tell the Difference in Norwalk, OH

Both grow on damp surfaces, but they look different and call for different responses. Here is a simple way to tell them apart.

☎ (567) 406-9153See all services
By the Norwalk Water Damage Pros team

People use the words mold and mildew almost interchangeably. In practice, mildew is a name for certain surface growths on damp plants and household surfaces, and mold is the broader term for fungal growth that can go deeper and spread through materials. The difference matters because it changes how much you can do yourself.

Neither word tells you the exact organism, and only testing can. What you can do at home is use appearance, location and texture to decide how worried to be.

How they usually look

Mold and mildew compared
FeatureMildewMold
TextureFlat, powdery or downyFuzzy, slimy or raised
ColorWhite, gray or yellowishGreen, black, brown, blue or white
WhereSurface of damp tile, windowsills, shower curtainsDrywall, wood, carpet, insulation and deeper layers
SpreadStays on the surfaceCan grow into materials
CleaningOften removed with detergent and a scrubMay require removal of the material

The table is a general guide. A dark patch on bathroom caulk may be either, and the safest assumption is that any growth means moisture is present.

Where each tends to show up

  • Mildew: shower tiles, grout, window tracks, damp fabric and basement walls with light films
  • Mold: behind wallpaper, under carpet pad, in wall cavities, on ceiling tiles and in insulation
  • Both: cold exterior walls, closets against outside walls and rooms with poor ventilation

A musty smell with nothing visible often points to hidden mold. That is a reason to check for moisture before buying cleaners.

What to do about each

Mildew on a hard surface

Scrub with detergent and water, rinse, dry and increase ventilation.

Mold on a small hard surface

Clean carefully with protection, and watch for a return.

Mold in drywall, pad or insulation

Plan on removal, not cleaning.

Anything large or hidden

Call a professional for containment and assessment.

EPA guidance suggests that mold areas of roughly 10 square feet or more are beyond homeowner cleaning. In either case, drying the space and fixing the moisture source are the real solution.

Winter condensation in Norwalk

Norwalk averages about 33 inches of snow a year in the NOAA 1991 to 2020 normals, and its record low is minus 25 degrees Fahrenheit. In cold weather, warm indoor air condenses on window glass and cold walls, which is a common source of mildew on sills and window frames. Wiping condensation off in the morning and running a bathroom fan keeps the film from building.

In summer, basements and crawl spaces are the damp areas. A hygrometer showing readings above 60 percent for long stretches is a sign that a dehumidifier is needed.

When mildew means something more

Repeated mildew in one spot can signal a hidden moisture problem, such as a leak behind tile or a poorly vented bathroom. If it returns after cleaning, treat it like mold and look behind it. Whatever you call it, damp surfaces are the real problem.

Where each one tends to show up

Mildew is a surface growth, usually flat and powdery or downy, and often gray or white. It shows up on shower walls, window sills and damp fabrics. Mold covers a wider group, can be fuzzy or slimy, and can be black, green, orange or white.

Color is not a reliable guide. Two molds of the same color can behave differently, and the same species can look different in different lighting. Do not try to identify it by eye to decide if it is dangerous.

What to do about either one

The response is the same in both cases: find the moisture, stop it and dry the area. Cleaning without fixing the water source only brings the growth back. A small patch on a hard surface can be wiped, while growth on drywall, insulation or wood is a different job.

People with asthma, allergies or weak immune systems should avoid cleaning mold themselves. The mold remediation page explains how professionals contain larger areas, and Milan and Willard callers can describe their problem by phone first.

Humidity matters too. Indoor humidity that stays high for days gives both growths what they need. A simple hygrometer costs little and tells you whether a basement or bathroom needs a dehumidifier or better ventilation.

Questions homeowners ask

Is mildew less serious than mold?

Mildew stays on the surface and is easier to clean, but both signal moisture. If either comes back, look for a hidden source.

Can I use the same cleaner for both?

On hard surfaces, detergent and water work for small areas of either. Porous materials with growth in them are removed.

When is testing useful?

Testing can help identify hidden growth or provide before and after documentation. It is not required for visible growth that you plan to remove.
