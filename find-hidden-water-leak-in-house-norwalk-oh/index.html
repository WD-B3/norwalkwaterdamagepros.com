How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your House in Norwalk, OH

Most hidden leaks give clues. The water meter, your bill and a few quick tests can narrow it down before you call anyone.

☎ (567) 406-9153See all services
By the Norwalk Water Damage Pros team

A hidden leak is any leak you cannot see, inside a wall, under a floor, in a ceiling or underground between the meter and the house. They matter because they run without interruption, and the damage and the water bill grow together.

This guide lists tests you can do in an hour with no special tools. They will not find every leak, but they tell you whether one exists and often which part of the house it is in.

Start with the meter

Water meters have a small dial or a flow indicator that moves when any water passes. Turn off every faucet and appliance, make sure no one flushes a toilet and read the meter. Wait 30 minutes with no water use and read it again. If the numbers changed, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and your fixtures.

Shut everything off

Faucets, appliances, ice makers and irrigation.

Read the meter

Note the dial or the digits.

Wait

Thirty minutes to two hours, with no water use.

Read again

Any change means a leak.

A toilet flapper is the most common hidden leak, and it can waste a surprising amount of water without a sound. Replacing the flapper is a small inexpensive repair.

Signs of a leak inside walls and floors

  • A musty smell in a room with no visible water
  • Paint that bubbles or peels in one patch
  • Warm spots on a floor over a slab
  • The sound of running water when everything is off
  • Baseboards that swell or stain

A thermal camera shows temperature differences from wet materials, and a moisture meter confirms them. If you find a wet wall, the wall and drywall page explains how it is opened and dried. A stain on a ceiling follows the steps on ceiling water damage repair.

Norwalk's water meter and the city

The city of Norwalk owns the municipal water system and bills customers through the Utility Billing Office at City Hall. Comparing several months of bills is a way to see when usage started to climb. A bill that jumps with no change in habits is worth a meter test.

The city's billing page notes that service changes require notice from the property owner. If the leak is on your side of the meter, the repair belongs to you. If you think it is on the street side, call the city's utility billing office and have a plumber confirm where the break is.

When to call a professional

If the meter moves and you cannot find the leak, a plumber can use acoustic and thermal equipment to find a line in a wall or under a slab. The slab leak page explains the signs of a leak beneath a concrete floor.

Once the leak is stopped, any wet material must be dried. EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold, and a leak that has run for a long time often means the materials are already past that window.

A moving meter with every tap off is the clearest signal there is.

Households in Collins and Milan that have private wells have no city meter, but a pressure tank that cycles when no water is used gives a similar hint.

When a hidden leak has already done damage

Finding the leak is only half of the job. Wet insulation, drywall and subfloor keep holding moisture after the pipe is fixed, and that is where mold starts. If you opened a wall or lifted a floor to find the source, leave it open and call for drying before anyone closes it up.

A plumber repairs the pipe, and a restoration crew dries what the water reached. They are separate trades, and a repair visit alone rarely dries the structure. Structural drying explains how readings show when a wall is truly dry, and the slab leak page covers leaks under a concrete floor.

Write down the meter reading and the time when you start any test. Numbers on paper are easier to share with a plumber than a general impression, and they show whether the leak is getting worse.

Questions homeowners ask

How much does a hidden leak raise a water bill?

It depends on the size of the leak and how long it runs. Any unexplained increase is worth a meter test.

Can I find a leak without tools?

Often, yes. The meter test, the toilet dye test and a walk around the house checking for stains and smells will find most leaks.

Who do I call about a leak between the meter and the street?

Call the city's utility billing office. A plumber can tell you whether the break is before or after the meter.
