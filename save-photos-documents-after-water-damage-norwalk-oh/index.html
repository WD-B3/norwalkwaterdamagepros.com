How to Save Photos and Important Documents After Water Damage in Norwalk, OH

Paper and photos do not fail at once. Speed, gentle handling and a freezer can save many of them.

☎ (567) 406-9153See all services
By the Norwalk Water Damage Pros team

Photos and papers are the items most people cannot replace. The good news is that wet paper does not turn to pulp immediately, and many photographs can be saved if they are handled gently and dried fast. The bad news is that mold starts in a day or two, so the window is short.

This guide covers photographs, paper documents and books. It follows common conservation advice used by libraries and archives, and it is general guidance.

The first hours

Check the water

Clean water is much safer to work with than flood or sewage water.

Handle gently

Wet paper tears easily, so support it from below.

Separate

Peel photos and pages apart before they dry together, only if they come apart easily.

Dry or freeze

Air dry thin items, and freeze the rest if you cannot dry them in a day or two.

If the water was contaminated, wear gloves and keep items away from food areas. Do not attempt to clean sewage-contaminated paper at home, but photograph it for your records.

Photographs

  • Rinse mud off with clean, cool water, gently, without touching the surface
  • Lay photos face up on clean paper towels or blotting paper, or hang them by a corner
  • Keep them out of direct sun, and use a fan with no heat
  • Do not stack wet photos, since the surfaces stick together
  • Freeze photographs that cannot be dried right away, in a sealed bag with wax paper between them

Scan or photograph important prints before cleaning if they are fragile. Digital copies are useful for insurance and for reprints.

Documents and books

Wet paper triage
ItemWhat to do
Loose papersAir dry on clean paper towels, or freeze
BooksStand upright with pages fanned, or freeze if soaked
Certificates and IDsDry flat, then contact the issuing agency for replacements
Documents with inkDry flat and avoid rubbing
Items touched by sewage or flood waterPhotograph and discard, and ask for replacements

Do not use an oven or a hair dryer on a heat setting, which can warp paper and damage prints. Slow, cool air drying is gentler.

Replacing documents in Huron County

For important records, think about replacements early. Many agencies issue replacements of birth certificates, titles and licenses, and the Huron County offices in Norwalk can say where to start. Records of property, such as deeds, are held by county offices, so a damaged copy is usually replaceable.

Basement storage is the usual loss. Norwalk's city GIS map shows wetland and floodplain locations, so if your lot is in or near a mapped area, store papers on upper shelves or in sealed plastic tubs.

Preventing the next loss

  • Store papers in sealed plastic tubs, not cardboard boxes
  • Keep important records on a shelf or in an upper floor
  • Scan key documents and keep copies off site
  • Check basement storage after heavy rain

After the items are safe, the structure still needs to be dried. Water extraction comes first and structural drying follows, and the EPA guidance to dry water-damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours applies to the room as well as the papers.

Papers that go in a freezer today can be dried carefully later.

Dry paper before it molds

Wet paper is fragile, so handle it by the edges and do not pull pages apart. Lay documents flat on clean towels or paper, change the paper as it soaks up water and keep them in a cool, dry room. Mold can start within a couple of days if the paper stays damp.

For a large stack, freezing buys time. Place the documents in freezer bags, label them and freeze them until you can sort them. Freezing stops mold growth, and a conservator or document restoration service can dry frozen paper properly later.

Photographs

Photos stuck together should be rinsed gently in clean water and laid face up on a clean surface to dry. Do not touch the image side. If prints are stuck fast, freezing them and asking a photo conservator is safer than pulling them apart.

Make a digital copy as soon as a photo is dry enough to handle. A phone picture is better than losing the image. Scanning can wait, but the copy cannot.

Important papers and the next step

Passports, deeds, titles and insurance papers are worth drying first. Ask the issuing office about replacing anything that cannot be saved. Meanwhile, the building itself needs drying too, which is covered under structural drying, and Collins and Willard residents can call the same line for help with the room.

Do not use a hair dryer, an oven or a microwave on wet papers or photos. Heat can curl, warp and fuse them, and slow air drying at room temperature is gentler.

Questions homeowners ask

Can wet photos be saved?

Often, if they are rinsed gently and dried quickly, or frozen until you can dry them. Modern prints survive better than old ones, but do not wait.

Should I freeze wet documents?

Yes, if you cannot dry them within a day or two. Freezing stops mold and gives you time to dry them carefully.

Who replaces destroyed certificates in Norwalk?

The issuing agency does. Huron County offices in Norwalk can point you to where to start for local records.
